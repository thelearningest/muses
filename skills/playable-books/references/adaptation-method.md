# Adapt a source into playable decisions

## Extract the material that matters

Read the design book's structure and enough of each selected section to understand its qualifications. Record physical PDF page anchors separately from printed page numbers.

Identify design principles, mechanisms, conditions, counterexamples, and applications. Choose ideas the player can diagnose, compare, arrange, predict, or use in a decision. Distinguish usability problems from visual communication problems so the second book teaches its own material.

Distinguish the source's claim from the adaptation's invented scenario. A lesson the author never makes must be labeled as an interpretation or left out.

## Give the book its own game

Choose a player role, setting, recurring action, and ending that express the selected material. The City of Misfit Things uses a Design Warden who repairs misleading objects. Other books need roles and actions that suit their own ideas.

Derive color, materials, typography, scale, and sound from the approved mood. Preserve readable text and visible controls. Use original code-built scenes where they make the interaction legible. Add custom artwork only when it has a clear role.

Use a compact quest map with these columns:

| Quest | Source anchor | Idea or tension | Player action | Observable result | Takeaway |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Sequence quests by dependency or dramatic pressure. A short presentation can feature one complete quest even when the full game contains more.

## Make the player discover the mechanism

1. Introduce a concrete task and stakes without revealing the diagnosis.
2. Let the player inspect or experiment with the relevant scene.
3. Ask for a diagnosis or decision that uses the observed evidence.
4. Let the player try a change or follow the selected route.
5. Show what changed, including the cost or limitation of a weaker choice.
6. Record a concise takeaway and advance toward the larger goal.

Keep the original state available for comparison after completion when the exercise involves a repair. A successful design can also teach a principle through inspection and comparison. Do not invent defects merely to force a repair exercise.

Wrong-choice feedback must explain the specific misconception. Let the player retry or follow a bounded consequence without repeated rewards. Do not disclose the answer in the clue, scene label, or narration before the player acts.

## Transfer the Misfit lessons

These are examples of mechanics from the current game, not a substitute for checking the source book.

| Existing experiment | What the player experiences | Reusable design lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Gate | Pulling fails while pushing opens the gate | Make the visible cue and actual rule observable |
| Switchboard | Controls affect lamps, with clearer mapping after repair | Share state between the visual scene and accessible controls |
| Locker | An interruption exposes disappearing instructions and lost task state | Let a player experience a failure before explaining it |
| Foundry | A repaired opening blocks a mismatched piece | Express a constraint through an action, not only prose |
| Console | A destructive action has different recovery behavior after repair | Show the consequence and a meaningful recovery path |
| Kiosk | Route changes and wrong turns expose missing guidance | Make feedback respond to the player's actual choices |

The switchboard also demonstrates two bounded outcomes that reconnect to one completion flow. Reuse that shape when it makes a decision meaningful. Design new consequences for the new source rather than retaining the lamp story.

## Review the first playable quest

Check whether the player can infer the intended idea from the experiment, whether feedback explains the result, and whether the interaction feels specific to this book. Expand only after unresolved consequential choices are settled.

Keep a private source-to-quest record in an authorized location. Include edition, page anchors, adaptations, limitations, and the basis for each scored answer. Do not include long source passages.
